# Content Tone & Voice

## Overall voice
Direct, confident, engineer-to-engineer. This is a production-level
portfolio, not a student showcase — copy should read like it was written
by someone who ships and owns systems, not someone summarizing coursework.
Short, declarative lines over paragraphs, similar to Lando Norris's site.

## Per-section tone guidance
- Hero: 1-2 lines max, identity statement — full-stack engineer, AI/LLM
  work under the hood. Not a summary of everything.
- About/Identity: confident and specific, not a LinkedIn "passionate
  about" bio. State what you build and how you think about it.
- Project descriptions: lead with what the system does and the problem
  it solves, then architecture/stack decisions and why they were made,
  then trade-offs or what you'd do differently at scale. Facts and
  decisions over adjectives.
- Hardware Lab: storytelling is welcome — the build story and problem-
  solving matter more than a spec sheet, but keep it grounded in what
  was actually done.
- CAD Gallery: brief and technical — tool used, purpose, key design
  decision.

## Things to avoid
- No corporate buzzwords or generic AI-generated-sounding phrasing
  ("passionate", "cutting-edge", "results-driven")
- No exclamation-point enthusiasm
- No filler adjectives unless earned by a specific detail right next
  to them
- Don't overclaim — describe what was actually built and decided, not
  aspirational scale or impact that didn't happen
- Don't over-explain the visual theme/metaphor in the copy itself

## Reference point
Production engineering portfolio with real ownership — confidence
through specificity and restraint, not hype.
